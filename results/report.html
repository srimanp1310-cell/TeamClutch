The measurement overshot its own ceiling

At S=512, attention is 14.3% of the forward pass's arithmetic, so making it infinitely fast could yield at most 1.167×. We measured 2.414×. Inverting Amdahl, that requires attention to have held 59% of the runtime — 4.1× its share of the arithmetic. A region cannot consume triple its arithmetic share unless it is waiting on something other than arithmetic. This is direct evidence the cost was memory traffic: writing and re-reading the [B, H, S, S] score matrix, not the two matrix multiplies.

The ridge moves right as precision drops

Reduced precision raises the compute roof and does nothing at all for the bandwidth roof, so the crossover shifts from 63 to about 133 FLOP/byte. Switching to a smaller dtype can therefore make a workload more memory-bound, not less — the kernel does not simply move up the chart, it can cross to the other side of the ridge and change which optimization comes next.

04 Rung 3 · abandoned

Reduced precision cannot be shipped at depth

Error compounds through the residual stream. By the benchmark's default of six layers, fp32 is the only dtype left.

dtype1 layer2 layers 6 layers · the defaultwhat the numbers say
fp32passpasspassmax_abs 0.0075 at 6 layers — 7.5× the atol budget, carried by the relative leg
fp16passfailfail2.020× at one layer; 0.0059 → 0.0078 (4 ULP) by two and six
bf16failfailfailworst element is 2 ULP = 1.44% relative, against a 1% tolerance
This is the benchmark's constraint, not a defect

The reference rounds softmax probabilities to the working dtype before the PV matmul; a fused kernel keeps them in fp32 and is therefore more accurate — it simply does not reproduce the reference's intermediate rounding. At magnitude 2.17 a 1% relative tolerance is 1.39 ULP of bf16, tighter than the format's own granularity. Only an implementation reproducing the reference's operation order bit-for-bit can pass, which is exactly what an optimized kernel must not do.
